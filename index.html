The workshop

For many people in Stradbally, Abdel is known through his workshop — a place associated with cars, panel beating, practical problem-solving and local connection.

"His shed has become something of a local hub — friends frequently stop by, and increasingly, Irish locals do too."Niall Creery, Chairman, Stradbally GAA Football Committee
"He's become a part of the local scene... you'd often see people stopping by to visit or just to catch up with him."Pat Deegan, shed owner

Those who know Abdel describe him as hardworking, generous with his time and willing to share tools, knowledge and advice. His workshop represents more than employment or enterprise; it represents belonging, routine and trust.

The ducks by the river

Several supporters speak about Abdel's work ethic. Others speak about his kindness. One simple story captures this well: when ducks began visiting the river beside his workshop, Abdel began feeding them. They returned each day and were later joined by ducklings. He continued to look after them, buying food especially for them.

"When a pair of ducks showed up one day, he began feeding them. They now return every day, joined recently by a group of ducklings, and Abdel buys food especially for them."Niall Creery reference
Abdel feeding the ducks by the river beside his workshop.

Football, friendship and community

One of the clearest examples of Abdel's integration into Irish life is his involvement with the Stradbally Dads & Lads Gaelic Football group.

Stradbally Dads & Lads

David Walsh, coordinator of the group, described Abdel as attending every training session after joining, becoming a very effective corner forward, being keen to learn about the sport and its history, and integrating extremely well into the group and the local community.

A teammate, not a visitor

Niall Creery described Abdel as bringing a jovial spirit and a great sense of humour, someone who enjoys the craic and fits right in. The photographs below show him standing naturally alongside teammates and families as part of the group.

"He has integrated extremely well into the group and the local community and he would be a big loss to us all if he cannot stay with us."David Walsh, Stradbally Dads & Lads coordinator
"Polite, mannerly, sociable and getting along well with everyone."Austin Clancy, Peace Commissioner
